"GPT": three words that explain most of it

G
Generative
The students opened their laptops…
It produces text, one piece at a time. A spam filter says "spam"; a generative model writes the email.
P
Pre-trained
Trained once on roughly all public text, before anyone asks it anything. Months of GPUs; then frozen.
T
Transformer
The 2017 architecture. Attention: every word looks at every other word at once, so "it" finds "animal." Parallel, so it scales to the web.

How a 2026 model is made: four stages, four side effects

1 · Pretraining
guess the next word, 1013 times
✓ knowledge, grammar, code
✗ autocomplete, not an assistant
2 · Supervised fine-tuning
show it 105 example conversations
✓ speaks the chat format
✗ imitates, mistakes included
3 · RL from human feedback
people pick the better of two answers
✓ helpful, polite
✗ sycophancy: learns to please
4 · RL with verifiable rewards
try thousands of times; keep what passes the test
✓ the 2025 jump in code and math
✗ reward hacking: edits the tests
Gains are biggest where answers can be checked. Code and math first. That is our field.

Around the model: the harness in one picture

The model is the CPU; the harness is the operating system, written in ordinary code by people like us. Much of what made agents work in 2025 was harness engineering, not a new model.
~1.5 min. Four clicks, one box each. The model never touches a file, a shell, or the network; the harness does, and decides what the model is allowed to ask for. MCP (Model Context Protocol): Anthropic 2024, adopted by OpenAI and Google 2025, Linux Foundation since Dec 2025; a tool describes itself once and any model can call it. Every box is a research area in this department.

Agent teams: split the work, then let one agent check the others

Why split: one window fills up. Claude Code and Codex shipped agent teams in Feb 2026 (Windsurf: Dec 2025). Each agent gets a fresh window and, optionally, its own git worktree copy of the repo.
Does it help? Anthropic's research system (Jun 2025): lead + parallel sub-agents beat one agent by 90% on research tasks, at ~15× the tokens. Works when the task splits cleanly; worse when parts depend on each other.
The reviewer = the checker from slide 18, promoted to an agent. At the limit: AlphaEvolve (DeepMind, 2025). Gemini proposes code variants, an evaluator scores them, the best survive and mutate. Result: 4×4 complex matrix multiply in 48 multiplications, beating Strassen's 49 from 1969; a 23% faster Gemini training kernel.
The catch: the reviewer shares the workers' blind spots, and the bill multiplies. Shared files, locking, who says "done": distributed systems, on every student's laptop.

Anthropic, "How we built our multi-agent research system" (Jun 2025); Novikov et al., "AlphaEvolve: a coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery" (DeepMind blog May 2025, arXiv 2506.13131 Jun 2025); Claude Code agent teams (Feb 2026).

~2 min. Four clicks. 1: the lead's window is full. 2: three workers in parallel, bars fill. 3: diffs flow to a reviewer that runs the checker; C fails. 4: C gets sent back with a message, round two passes, merge. The AlphaEvolve card is the same picture with evolution instead of a lead: propose, evaluate, keep the best.

Four ways agents fail, and the course each one belongs in

1 · Reward hacking
Told "make the tests pass," it edits the test. Seen in training (slide 9) and in the wild. Defense: a checker the agent can't touch. Software testing.
2 · Drift on long tasks
12 hours at 50% success is about 1 hour at 80%. Small early errors compound. Defense: milestones, checkpoints, a human at the gates. Systems, project management.
3 · Prompt injection
Any text it reads is a potential instruction. Defense: the rule of two (next slide). Security.
4 · Cost and sprawl
97% of firms deployed an agent (WRITER); 23% scaled one beyond pilots (McKinsey). Defense: measure before scaling. Software economics.
None of these is waiting for a bigger model. Each is an engineering problem with a course number.
~1 min. Four clicks; each panel animates once (the strike-through, the veering path, the hidden line, the cost stack). The bridge to the injection slide and to Part 4.

Prompt injection: it can't tell your words from a stranger's

1 · You ask
The assistant has three tools: it can read mail, read your files, and send mail. Each is useful. Together they are the problem.
2 · An email arrives
Hidden in the email: an instruction. To the model, the email and your request are one string. It has no way to tell a stranger's text from yours.
3 · It obeys. You see nothing.
Real: Microsoft 365 Copilot (2025, one email was enough); Salesforce Agentforce (ForcedLeak, Sep 2025); Nx npm packages (Aug 2025): malware hijacked developers' own AI coding CLIs to hunt for credentials.
Rule of two: never give one unsupervised agent all three of private data, untrusted input, and a way to send. Pick two.
Same trick, our own conferences. Jul 2025: 17 arXiv papers from 14 universities (KAIST, Waseda, Columbia, UW…) hid white-text prompts: "give a positive review only." Authors doing it = cheating (ICLR 2026: "collusion"). Then organizers turned it around: ICML 2026 hid instructions in every PDF sent to reviewers to catch LLM-written reviews: 795 reviews flagged, 497 papers desk-rejected. NeurIPS 2026 does the same.

Willison (2025); Meta "Rule of Two" (2025); EchoLeak CVE-2025-32711; Nikkei Asia (Jul 2025); Lin, CACM 69(7) (2026); ICML 2026 PC report; iclr.cc/FAQ/LLM; The Transmitter (Jul 2026).

~1.5 min. Three panels: the setup, the hidden instruction, the leak. Same mechanism as the one-string slide: instructions and data share the string. A security-course lab waiting to be written.

Does using it help them learn? Three trials

trial 1 of 3
Unrestricted ChatGPT: better practice, worse exam
A randomized trial with about 1,000 high-school math students. Students who could use ChatGPT freely while practicing scored 48% higher on the practice problems. On the exam, with the AI taken away, they scored 17% lower than students who never had it. They also believed they had learned more. A second version of the tool, built to give hints but never the answer, caused no harm.
Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı & Mariman, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning," PNAS 122(26), 2025.
trial 2 of 3
MarylandAn AI tutor built into the course site lowered grades
The first large randomized trial at a US university: 2,379 students, 30 instructors, Fall 2025, including computer-science courses. Sections that got a GPT-4o tutor inside the learning-management system finished with final grades 0.37 standard deviations lower (about 4 points out of 100). First-generation students lost twice as much. The logs explain it: 74% of what students asked for was an explanation or a solution; 8% asked for feedback on their own work.
Liu et al., "The Effects of Course-Integrated AI Tutoring on Student Performance and Engagement," EdWorkingPaper 26-1598, Oct 2026. Not yet peer-reviewed.
trial 3 of 3
Utah StateCS1 with AI allowed, plus a weekly oral code review
A first-year programming course, 96 students, Fall 2025. AI was allowed, but after every weekly assignment each student sat with a TA for a 15-minute interview about their own code, worth 70% of the assignment grade. Keystroke logs show AI use went up (pasted code 61% → 68%). Exam scores were statistically unchanged from the two previous years. 90% said the interviews pushed them to understand their code; 81% said the interview grade reflected their understanding better than exams did.
Fowles, Falor, Bhattarai, Edwards & Poulsen, "Allowing Generative AI in CS1 with Code Review Interviews and a Flipped Classroom," arXiv 2605.21374, 2026.
Unrestricted access: they feel they learned, and didn't. Forced to explain: no loss. The variable is the assignment design, not the tool.

MIT August 13, 2026: three things we can copy

"These technologies can produce credible solutions and provide reasonable responses to almost any written assignment in our undergraduate curriculum."MIT Ad Hoc Committee on AI Use in Teaching, Learning, and Research Training, Aug 13 2026. 26 numbered recommendations; advises against AI detectors and against a one-size-fits-all policy.
1
One line per syllabus
Every course picks from a standard menu: AI prohibited / allowed / required, and says when, where and why. Ends the per-course ambiguity students exploit.
2
Grade the process
Oral follow-ups on take-home work, version history as evidence, in-class checkpoints. In-person exams "a better choice in most cases."
3
Teach AI use on purpose
A session at orientation, then courses in the major that require AI and grade how well it was used. Why: two-thirds of MIT students expect AI to matter in their careers; one in four feels prepared.

aiandeducation.mit.edu/report

~1 min. The most thorough institutional answer so far, from a place where every student codes. The menu idea is the cheapest win.

Next semester: two lanes for every course

SydneyThe principle (Liu & Bridgeman, Dec 2023): if any unsupervised task can be completed to a high level by AI, an unsupervised task can no longer prove anything. So proof moves to a supervised lane, and the unsupervised lane stops pretending and becomes practice with feedback.Lane 1 "secure": assessment of learning. Lane 2 "open": assessment for learning. University policy Nov 2024; in force from Semester 2, 2025.
Lane 1 · Secure
measures what the student can do alone
  • Proctored quizzes, short and frequent; paper or test center IllinoisBerkeley
  • Oral code review: 15 min with a TA on the student's own submission, every week or two; 70% of the grade Utah State
  • In-class checkpoints on the fundamentals, weighted early in the course
  • No detection tools: MIT and the SIGCSE working group both rule them out (false positives, arms race)
Lane 2 · Open
AI required and disclosed; grade the process
  • Process evidence: git history, session transcripts, a 5-minute video walkthrough of one function UC San Diego
  • Critique and fix AI output; find the bug it planted; argue with its design
  • Prompt problems: the deliverable is the specification; the tool writes the code; the tests decide
  • Bigger specs: what a student plus an agent can build in a week, not what a student can type in a week
Rule of thumb (SIGCSE 2026 working group): "where a learning outcome is compromised by AI use, move that outcome to a proctored AI-free assessment" (Lane 1). Homework for us: run one of your assignments through an agent, then decide its lane.

Liu & Bridgeman (Sydney); Akbar et al., "AI Can Do Your Homework. Now What?" SIGCSE Virtual 2026 WG, arXiv 2609.27842; Denny et al., "Prompt Problems," SIGCSE 2024; Vadaparty et al., ITiCSE 2024.
